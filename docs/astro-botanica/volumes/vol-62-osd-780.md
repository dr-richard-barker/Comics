## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **Jess M. Bunchek (SURA / KSC & University of Bremen Lead Author):** Botanist and agronomist leading the `VEG-04A`/`VEG-04B` synthesis—tracking how light spectrum, fertilizer salt stress, and cut-and-come-again harvests reshape mizuna yield, flavor, and microbial load.

- **Dr. Gioia D. Massa, Matthew W. Romeyn & Raymond M. Wheeler (NASA KSC Veggie Science Team):** Principal investigator and co-leads; down-selected mizuna after `'Tokyo Bekana'` Chinese cabbage showed `CO₂` stress, and redesigned `VEG-04B` watering and `T70` fertilizer after `VEG-04A` plant losses.

- **Mary E. Hummerick & LaShelle E. Spencer (Noetic Strategies Microbiology & Horticulture):** Lead the `203` `TSA`/`IMA` plate-count assays, `ProSan` wipe validation, and `WGS` confirmation of microbial isolates from leaves, pillows, and Veggie bellows.

- **Grace L. Douglas, Millennia Young, Robert C. Morrow & Cary A. Mitchell:** JSC Space Food Systems & biostatistics leads (`n = 14` flight vs. `n = 79` ground sensory panels), Sierra Space hardware architect, and Purdue LED photobiology pioneer.

- **The Dual Mizuna Canopies (`90%R:10%B` vs. `50%R:50%B`):** *Brassica rapa* var. *japonica* growing under white **Beta Cloth** shades—balancing glucosinolate spice, mineral accumulation, and `> 80%` Veggie chamber humidity.

- **The Phyllosphere & Surface Microbiota (`Ralstonia`, `Fusarium`, `Staphylococcus aureus`):** Bacterial and fungal communities shaped by `89.8–91.9%` chamber RH, repeated cutting exudates, and crew handling (`gloves` in orbit vs. `lab coats + hair/beard caps + gloves` on Earth).

## 3. 32-Page Chapter Script

### Act I: Ninety-Ten vs. Fifty-Fifty (Pages 1–10)

**Page 1**

- **Panel 1 (Full-width establishing shot):** Two **Veggie** chambers glow side by side inside the International Space Station during `Expedition 59` (`June 6, 2019`): the left bellows blazes deep **crimson-magenta (`90% Red : 10% Blue`, `270:30:30`)**, while the right glows **electric violet-blue (`50% Red : 50% Blue`, `150:150:30`)**.

- **Panel 2 (Close-up, pillow top):** A square of **white Beta Cloth** (`Dunmore Aerospace`) covers each black Veggie pillow, leaving only the germination wick slot exposed.

- **Caption (Narrator):** *Equal photon counts don't carry equal heat. Blue-rich LEDs dump `15%` more energy into black pillow fabric than red-rich LEDs—until white Beta Cloth blocks `62%` of the glare.*

- **Panel 3 (Two-shot, KSC):** Gioia Massa and Jess Bunchek check the `VEG-04A` downlink photos.

- **Gioia Massa:** "`Tokyo Bekana'` cabbage stressed under ISS `CO₂`. Mizuna mustard won the crop screen—now let's see what `90:10` versus `50:50` light does to yield, nutrition, and flavor."

**Page 2**

- **Panel 1 (Timeline & Prep):** `VEG-04A` (`6` pillows per chamber, `250 mL` `1:1` `Turface` arcillite + `7.5 g/L T70 + 5 g/L T180 Nutricote 18-6-8`) launches on `SpaceX-16` / `18` prep cycles; `48` hours behind orbit, KSC ground chambers replay real-time ISS temperature (`~22.4°C`), `40%` ambient RH, and `2000–3000 ppm` CO₂.

- **Panel 2 (Day 3 & Day 7):** Crew members open the pillow wicks at `3` DAI, photograph every watering, and thin seedlings at `7` DAI by snipping the stem base rather than pulling roots.

**Page 3**

- **Panel 1 (Crisis in `VEG-04A`, mid-June 2019):** Downlinked photos from ISS trigger alarm at KSC. Leaves curl and scorch at the margins; **one-third of flight plants** (`1/6` red-rich, `3/6` blue-rich) and **half of ground plants** (`3/6` red, `3/6` blue) die from early excess water and `T70` fertilizer salt stress!

- **Panel 2 (Complication):** Black pillow blanks placed over the empty slots absorb extra heat, stressing the survivors further and forcing `VEG-04A` to stretch to **`35` DAI** (`11 July 2019`) before terminal harvest.

**Page 4**

- **Panel 1 (KSC Troubleshooting Bench):** Between `VEG-04A` and `VEG-04B`, LaShelle Spencer and Matthew Romeyn overhaul the recipe in flight-like ground hardware.

- **LaShelle Spencer:** "Cut fast-release `T70` Nutricote by `47%`—from `7.5` down to **`4 g/L`**. Keep the white Beta Cloth shades on for the *entire* `58`-day run, replace dead pillows with white shades instead of black blanks, delay thinning to `13` DAI, and hold off watering the root mat until `16` DAI."

**Page 5**

- **Panel 1 (`VEG-04B` Launch & Run, Oct 3 – Nov 30, 2019):** The revised `VEG-04B` pillows thrive—`5/6` flight red-rich, `5/6` flight blue-rich, and `100%` of ground controls survive to enter **cut-and-come-again** sequential harvesting at **`29`**, **`43`**, and **`58` DAI** (`28, 42, 56` DAI on Earth).

- **Panel 2 (Orbital Taste Test):** Freshly snipped mizuna leaves are weighed on the `Mass Measurement Device (MMD)`, pressed between **`1%` `ProSan` wipes** (`pH 2.9`) for `30` seconds, and tasted plain after a water-bag palate rinse.

**Page 6**

- **Panel 1 (Organoleptic Scorecard — *Bunchek et al.* Fig. 2):** Astronauts (`n = 14` flight evaluations) score all flight mizuna **higher** in overall acceptability than the `n = 79` Johnson Space Center ground panel, which rates Earth-grown mizuna noticeably more **bitter**.

- **Panel 2 (Light-recipe nuance):** In orbit, red-rich mizuna tastes slightly less bitter and less crisp than blue-rich; overall, both recipes earn enthusiastic crew approval.

**Pages 7–8 (Double-Page Spread Anchor: *The Photon Trade-Off — Yield vs. Minerals*)**

- **Left Page (Page 7) — *Biomass & Harvest Mode (`VEG-04A` vs. `VEG-04B`)*:** Side-by-side canopy silhouettes. In single-harvest `VEG-04A`, red-rich flight mizuna produces **`293 g`** (`n = 5`) vs. **`97 g`** in blue-rich (`n = 3`). In cut-and-come-again `VEG-04B`, blue-rich pulls ahead at **`222.85 g`** vs. **`186.80 g`** in red-rich—though regrowth rate drops sharply (`p ≤ 0.001`) after each cut.

- **Right Page (Page 8) — *Mineral & Antioxidant Ledger (`ICP-OES`, `ORAC`, Phenolics)*:** Blue-rich light and `58`-day `VEG-04B` growth boost **`Ca`** (`p ≤ 0.01`), **`Mg`** (`p ≤ 0.01`), and **`Mn`** (`p ≤ 0.01`), whereas single-harvest `VEG-04A` packs higher **phenolics** (`18–27 mg/g` DW, `p ≤ 0.001`) and **`Fe`** (`67–85 µg/g` DW, `p ≤ 0.001`). Spaceflight mizuna also concentrates more **`S`** (`p ≤ 0.05`/`0.01`), **`K`**, **`Na`**, **`Mg`**, and **`Fe`** than ground controls.

- **Spread Caption:** *Want maximum single-cut biomass and phenolics? Run red-rich for one month. Want sustained multi-harvest greens rich in magnesium and manganese? Run blue-rich—and watch the humidity climb.*

**Page 9**

- **Panel 1 (MELFI Return & Plate-Count Bench, `OSD-780` / `LSDS-128`):** Half of each ISS harvest returns at `-80°C` alongside `8` Veggie surface swabs (pillows/bungees, bellows top/middle/bottom, fan screen, outlet vent) and thawed pillows—`n = 207` samples total.

- **Panel 2 (Homogenization):** Leaf, wick, root, and substrate samples shake with `3–4 mm` sterile glass beads in `PBS` on the `OMNI BeadRuptor` (`5 m/s`, `2 × 30 s`), then plate in duplicate onto **Tryptic Soy Agar (`TSA`, `48 h` at `30°C`)** and **Inhibitory Mold Agar (`IMA`, `72–120 h` at `30°C`)**.

**Page 10**

- **Panel 1 (Humidity Log, `HOBO` sensors):** Mary Hummerick compares the `HOBO` humidity traces inside the Veggie bellows: while Earth chambers sit at `70–77%` day RH, flight Veggie chambers stay **above `80%`**, peaking at **`89.8%` day / `91.9%` night** in `VEG-04B` blue-rich!

- **Mary Hummerick:** "Above `80%` equilibrium relative humidity, fungi and phyllosphere bacteria wake up fast."

### Act II: The Harvest Escalation & The Skin Commensal (Pages 11–22)

**Page 11**

- **Panel 1 (Leaf Plate Counts — `VEG-04A`):** Ground control leaves carry significantly fewer bacteria and fungi than flight leaves under red-rich light. In flight `VEG-04A`, leaves average **`1.7 × 10⁶ CFU/g`** (red-rich) and **`5.2 × 10⁴ CFU/g`** (blue-rich).

- **Panel 2 (`ProSan` wipe test on Earth):** Ground pre- vs. post-`ProSan` cleaning shows no significant mean shift (`p > 0.05`), though uncleaned leaves harbor the highest individual outliers.

**Page 12**

- **Panel 1 (Leaf Plate Counts — `VEG-04B` Escalation):** Across the three sequential cuts of `VEG-04B`, microbial loads climb step by step: starting at **`≤ 100 CFU/g`** at Harvest `1`, rising through Harvest `2`, and surging by Harvest `3` to **`5.2 × 10⁴` (red) / `2.1 × 10⁴ CFU/g` (blue) bacteria** and **`1.2 × 10⁷` / `2.5 × 10⁵ CFU/g` fungi**.

- **LaShelle Spencer:** "Every snip leaks leaf sap onto the canopy, and every harvest brings gloved hands back inside the bellows."

**Page 13**

- **Panel 1 (Veggie Surface Swabs — Tables 4 & 5):** Across all `8` hardware swab zones, ground controls remain sparse (`25–1,448 CFU/swab` bacteria; `10–147 CFU/swab` fungi). On the ISS, `VEG-04A` red-rich averages **`1.52 × 10⁵ CFU/swab`** bacteria (`1.1 × 10⁶` on pillow/bungee location `2`) and `7,739 CFU/swab` fungi (`57,750` at location `3`).

- **Panel 2 (Biosafety contrast):** On Earth, technicians wear lab coats, hair/beard caps, and gloves inside a restricted chamber; in orbit, astronauts wear only gloves in a lived-in cabin.

**Page 14**

- **Panel 1 (Pathogen Screen Alert):** All `VEG-04A` and `VEG-04B` samples test **negative** for *E. coli* and *Salmonella* spp. But on `VEG-04B` flight **plant `#5`** (red-rich, front-left pillow), Harvest `2` yields **`88 CFU/g`** of **Staphylococcus aureus** (and positive detection at Harvest `3`).

- **Panel 2 (`Illumina MiSeq` WGS confirmation):** Mary Hummerick runs whole-genome sequencing and Average Nucleotide Identity (`ANI`): **`99.86%`** match to *S. aureus* `DSM20231` and **`99.85%`** to `NCTC 8325`—a common human skin commensal, orders of magnitude below the `10⁵–10⁸ CFU/mL` density needed for enterotoxin food poisoning.

**Page 15**

- **Panel 1 (Isolate Catalog):** `19` bacterial species in `VEG-04A` and `40` in `VEG-04B` (`5` shared): beneficial `Azospirillum lipoferum`, `Ralstonia pickettii`, `Sphingomonas parapaucimobilis`, `Microbacterium maritypicum`, alongside wheat leaf-spot pathogen *Clavibacter michiganensis* subsp. *tessellarius* and `6` fungal genera (*Fusarium oxysporum*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Purpureocillium*, *Rhodotorula mucilaginosa*, *Alternaria alternata*—with zero symptomatic leaf spot on mizuna).

**Page 16**

- **Panel 1 (Publication & Data Release):** `Bunchek et al. (2024)` (*Journal of Plant Interactions* `19:1`) and `OSD-780` (`LSDS-128`, `DOI: 10.26030/s3hn-cj93`, `207` samples) lock the `VEG-04` playbook into OSDR: red-rich for single-cut yield, blue-rich for multi-cut nutrition, white Beta Cloth for thermal balance, and strict hygiene for repeated harvests.

**Page 17**

- **Panel 1 (Quiet beat, KSC):** Raymond Wheeler looks from the `VEG-04` data sheets up to a framed Apollo-era photograph on the SSPF wall.

- **Raymond Wheeler:** "We've spent a decade perfecting orbital hardware—Veggie, `APH`, `XROOTS`. Yet one of the oldest questions in NASA's archive was asked before any of those chambers existed."

**Page 18**

- **Panel 1 (Archival transition):** A metallic sample canister labeled **Lunar Receiving Laboratory — Apollo 17** opens in monochrome flashback, revealing dark grey lunar fines resting beside a glass germination slide chamber.

- **Caption (Narrator):** *Fifty years before `OSD-750` tested lunar simulants on potatoes, NASA tested **real Moon dust** on cabbage, Brussels sprouts, radish, and lettuce.*

### Act III: Back to the Lunar Receiving Lab (`OSD-855`) (Pages 19–30)

**Page 19**

- **Panel 1 (Historical lab):** Inside the Lunar Receiving Laboratory at Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center, Pratt H. Johnson, Gene A. Bozarth, Charles H. Walkinshaw, S. Venketeswaran, and P. A. Covington prepare **"Challenge Units"**—glass slide chambers holding surface seeds of **cabbage, Brussels sprouts, radish, and lettuce** (`Tracheophyta`).

**Page 20**

- **Panel 1 (Three-way comparison):** Three treatments sprinkle onto the seeds before sterile water saturation:

  - **Lunar Material** (genuine **Apollo 17** regolith)

  - **Basalt** (terrestrial basaltic mixture resembling lunar rock)

  - **Iowa** (`105 µm`-sieved Iowa corn soil) + **Control** (no dust added).

**Page 21**

- **Panel 1 (Two questions):** Did lunar material carry any biological pathogen capable of causing plant disease? And how did real Apollo 17 dust alter seed germination and wet/dry seedling biomass?

**Page 22**

- **Panel 1 (Archive bridge):** Decades later, NASA OSDR curators rescue and standardize those historic **Apollo 17 Challenge System** germination records as **`OSD-855`**—preserving them alongside modern `SpaceX`-era Veggie (`OSD-780`) and `XROOTS` (`OSD-773`) datasets.

**Page 23**

- **Panel 1 (Visual rhyme):** A `250 mL` Veggie pillow of `1–2 mm` autoclaved `Turface` arcillite dissolves into a `105 µm`-sieved pinch of Apollo 17 lunar fines.

**Page 24**

- **Panel 1 (Continuity):** From `1972` Apollo 17 seed-challenge slides (`OSD-855`) to `2019` `VEG-04` LED mizuna (`OSD-780`) to `2025` `OS-927` potato simulants (`OSD-750`)—one unbroken line of space-agriculture inquiry.

**Page 25**

- **Panel 1 (Detail):** A `VEG-04B` mizuna leaf glows under `50:50` blue-rich light; beside it, a brassica cousin—cabbage and Brussels sprouts—germinates on an Apollo 17 glass slide.

**Page 26**

- **Panel 1 (Microbiology link):** On `VEG-04`, `TSA` and `IMA` tracked microbes across `207` flight and ground samples. On `OSD-855`, the original Apollo quarantine mandate asked whether Moon dust itself harbored anything infectious to Earth's crops.

**Page 27**

- **Panel 1 (Data stewardship note):** OSDR's open-science community call (`[redacted-email]`) invites researchers to help preserve historic Apollo 17 wet and dry biomass records where 1970s paper logs faded.

**Page 28**

- **Panel 1 (Final bridge):** `OSD-780` (`VEG-04A/B` mizuna) closes cleanly; the chronicle jumps back to the dawn of planetary botany at the **Lunar Receiving Laboratory**.

**Pages 29–30 (Cliffhanger Double-Page Spread: *Next Station — `OSD-855`*)**

- **Left Page (Page 29):** A monochrome-and-gold archival tableau of the **Johnson Space Center Lunar Receiving Laboratory**: gloved hands inside a containment cabinet dusting **Apollo 17 lunar regolith** over rows of **cabbage, Brussels sprouts, radish, and lettuce** seeds in glass slide chambers.

- **Right Page (Page 30):** Data overlay for **`OSD-855`**: *Apollo 17 Challenge System - Seed Germination*, comparing genuine **Lunar Material** vs. **Terrestrial Basalt**, **`105 µm` Iowa Corn Soil**, and **Control** for vascular-plant disease and wet/dry biomass.

### Left Spread — Page 31: `VEG-04A` vs. `VEG-04B` Flight Operations & Yield (`OSD-780` + `Bunchek et al., 2024`)

|---|---|---|---|
