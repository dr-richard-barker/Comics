## 1. Volume Metadata Header

- **Series:** *Roots in Orbit: The NASA OSDR Plant Chronicles*

- **Volume:** 63

- **Chapter Title:** *The Shadow of Taurus-Littrow: The Apollo 17 Challenge*

- **OSDR Accession:** `OSD-855` (Apollo 17 Challenge System - Seed Germination)

- **Primary Historical Reference / Publication:** Walkinshaw, C. H., et al. (1975). *Results of the Apollo 17 Plant Pathological and Seed Germination Evaluation*. NASA Manned Spacecraft Center / Lunar Receiving Laboratory (LRL) Technical Memorandum (NASA-TM-X-58134). Companion studies: `OSD-856` (Simulated lunar soil), `OSD-877` (Apollo 15/16 post-flight biomedical evaluation), `OSD-883` (Apollo 14 lunar surface fines).

- **Chronology Note:** Continues after Volume 62 (`OSD-780`, Veggie-04B Mizuna). Accessions `OSD-781` through `OSD-854` (rodent, human, and cell biology missions) are recorded in `skipped_accessions`. Advances directly to the historic Apollo Lunar Receiving Laboratory quarantine bioassay series.

- **Setting & Hardware:** NASA Manned Spacecraft Center (now Johnson Space Center, Houston, TX) — **Lunar Receiving Laboratory (LRL) Biological Barrier Quarantine Facility**. Specialized class-III biological safety cabinets under negative pressure, autoclave airlocks, and hermetically sealed stainless steel gloveboxes. Culture rigs: sterile glass Petri dishes (`100 mm`), whatman filter pads saturated with distilled water or nutrient solution, and precision germinators at `23°C ± 1°C` in total darkness vs. diurnal white fluorescent illumination.

- **Organism & Assay Scope:** Tracheophyta (Vascular Plants) bio-barrier challenge:
  - *Brassica oleracea* var. *capitata* (Cabbage, Round Dutch)
  - *Brassica oleracea* var. *gemmifera* (Brussels Sprouts, Long Island Improved)
  - *Raphanus sativus* (Radish, Early Scarlet Globe)
  - *Lactuca sativa* (Lettuce, Great Lakes & Black Seeded Simpson)
  Assays: Germination percentage, radicle emergence rate, hypocotyl elongation velocity, gross pathological lesion indexing, and morphological distortion screening following direct contact with pristine Apollo 17 lunar regolith fines (Sample 70011 / 72501).

## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **Squad Brassicaceae (Radish & Cabbage Vanguard):** *Raphanus sativus* and *Brassica oleracea*. The shock troops of the plant quarantine trials. Rapid germinators with thick hypocotyls, equipped with strong seed coats and glucosinolate defensive reserves.

- **Squad Asteraceae (Lettuce Cadets):** *Lactuca sativa*. Highly sensitive bio-indicators. Their delicate radicles and translucent root hairs serve as living canaries in the coal mine for potential lunar phytotoxins or exotic bio-hazards.

- **The Quarantine Custodian (Lunar Receiving Laboratory Glovebox 4):** A heavy, hermetically sealed glovebox of cold stainless steel and thick leaded glass. It represents humanity's first strict planetary protection barrier, built to ensure no pathogen from the Moon escapes to Earth, and no Earth microbe contaminates the pristine Apollo 17 samples.

- **The Lunar Fines (Apollo 17 Sample 72501):** Unweathered, pristine sub-millimeter dust scooped from the Valley of Taurus-Littrow. Jagged, razor-sharp glassy impact beads, titanium-rich ilmenite shards, and agglutinates completely untouched by water or oxygen.

- **Enzyme Vanguard (Amylase & Expansin Mobilizers):** Alpha/beta-amylases breaking down endosperm starch reserves and expansin enzymes loosening embryonic cell walls to drive radicle emergence through harsh, jagged mineral beds.

- **Dialogue (LRL Director):** "We know the Moon has no atmosphere, no water, no life. But planetary protection demands proof. If lunar dust harbors a toxic agent or pathogen that can kill Earth's vegetation, our agricultural biosphere is at stake."

- **Panel 1:** The Apollo 17 Sample Vacuum Box is unsealed under dry nitrogen. A stainless steel spatula lifts a powdery, charcoal-gray dusting of Apollo 17 fines.

**Page 4**

- **Panel 2:** Sterile glass Petri dishes lined with double-layered filter paper pads. Distilled water is added. One cohort receives pristine Apollo 17 lunar fines dusted directly across the seeds; the other serves as the sterile terrestrial control.

**Page 5**

- **Panel 1:** Squad Radish (*Raphanus sativus*) takes position. One hundred seeds counted under a magnifying glass, placed in concentric circles on the moistened pad.

- **Panel 2:** A fine gray veil of Taurus-Littrow dust is sifted over the seeds. The dark mineral grains settle onto the dry, wrinkled seed coats.

**Page 6**

- **Panel 1:** Squad Lettuce (*Lactuca sativa*) enters Chamber 2. Extremely fragile seed coats. The razor-sharp micro-shards of lunar glass rest directly against the micropyle.

- **Panel 2:** The dishes are loaded into light-tight incubator vaults calibrated to a steady `23°C`. The countdown begins: Hour 0.

- **Caption:** *Three billion years of lunar dormancy meets four hundred million years of vascular plant evolution. The challenge is underway.*

- **SFX:** *KISHI — SEED COAT RUPTURE.*
