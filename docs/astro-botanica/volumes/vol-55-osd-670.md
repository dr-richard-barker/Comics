## 1. Volume Metadata Header

- **Series:** *Roots in Orbit: The NASA OSDR Plant Chronicles*

- **Volume:** 55

- **Chapter Title:** *The Carbonaceous Crust*

- **OSDR Accession:** `OSD-670` (Version 3 | `ALSDA ID: LSDS-73` | DOI: `10.26030/m04e-p031` | License: `CC0-1.0` | Size: `34.12 MB` | Submitted: `05-Oct-2023` | Initial Release: `21-May-2024` | Updated: `20-Mar-2025`)

- **Primary Publication:** Russell, S. J., Fieber-Beyer, S. K., & Yurkonis, K. A. (2022). *CI Asteroid Regolith as an In Situ Plant Growth Medium for Space Crop Production*. *The Planetary Science Journal*, `3(7):155` (UND Scholarly Commons | DOI: `10.3847/PSJ/ac74c9`, published `12-Jul-2022`)

- **Skipped Accessions:** `OSD-659` through `OSD-669` (contain no plant studies; `OSD-670` directly follows `OSD-658` in the plant chronology)

- **Hardware & Environment:** Ground study at the **University of North Dakota (UND)** Department of Biology, funded by the **North Dakota Space Grant Consortium**. Crops grew for `55 days` in a `Percival AR-66L` growth chamber (`19.8°C–23.4°C` diurnal ramp, `50–60% RH`, ambient CO2, `16 h / 8 h` photoperiod with `32 W` / `14 W` cool white fluorescent + `25 W` incandescent lamps up to `275 µmol/m²/s`, watered every other day to field capacity with distilled water). **CLASS Exolith Lab** (UCF) **CI asteroid regolith simulant** (modeled on the **Orgueil** CI carbonaceous chondrite) was tested against `SunShine` sphagnum peat moss, perlite, and commercial topsoil/vermiculite controls, plus a `2D` clinostat (`1 rotation/24 h` at `90°`). Quantitative soil chemistry was assayed at **Kansas State University Soil Testing Laboratory**.

- **Organism & Assay Scope:** Three space-crop species (`n = 83` sample records, `n = 39` photography assays): romaine lettuce (*Lactuca sativa* cv. `"Outredgeous"`), radish (*Raphanus sativus* cv. `"Pink Celebration"`), and pepper (*Capsicum annuum* cv. `"Chimayo"`), sown `5` seeds per `4-inch` pot at `~2.54 cm` depth. Measurements at `55 DAP`: germination, plant height (`cm`), dry biomass (`60°C` for `64 h`, `mg`), and `ImageJ` leaf area (`cm²`) from `iPhone SE (2nd gen)` (`28 mm`) photographs.

## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **The Orgueil CI Asteroid Regolith Simulant (`CLASS Exolith Lab`):** A primordial carbonaceous chondrite analog (`48.0 wt%` Mg-serpentine, `13.5%` magnetite, `9.0%` vermiculite, `7.5%` olivine, `6.5%` pyrite, `6.0%` epsomite, `5.0%` sub-bituminous coal, `5.0%` attapulgite). Classified as a silt-based medium (`68%` silt, `26%` sand, `6%` clay) with alkaline `pH 8.1`, low `CEC` (`8.15 meq/100 g`), and high `Mg:Ca` ratio (`6,927.0` vs. `1,779.9 mg/kg`).

- **`SunShine` Sphagnum Peat Moss (`100:0` Amendment):** Acidic organic matter (`pH 4.7`, `CEC 43.98 meq/100 g`, `87.6%` organic matter) that buffers the simulant's alkalinity, boosts nitrogen (`NO3 82.4` / `NH4 129.5 mg/kg`), and physically prevents serpentine-silt crusting.

- **Radish (*Raphanus sativus* cv. `"Pink Celebration"`):** The taproot canary in the asteroid mine. Achieving `78%` overall germination, it is the only crop whose height (`4.1 cm` → `0.65 cm`) and biomass (`95.0 mg` → `15.4 mg`) collapse linearly as simulant concentration rises—choked by compaction and `P` deficiency.

- **Romaine Lettuce (*Lactuca sativa* cv. `"Outredgeous"`):** The ISS Veggie veteran (`53%` average germination). Its height and biomass hold steady across surviving treatments, yet its leaf area shrinks linearly from `2.2 cm²` (`100:0`) to `0.35 cm²` (`50:50`).

- **Pepper (*Capsicum annuum* cv. `"Chimayo"`):** The resilient opposite-leaved fruit crop (`55%` germination, `3.8 cm` mean height, `16.4 mg` biomass), showing no statistically significant drop in height, leaf area, or biomass across surviving peat-simulant ratios.

## 3. 32-Page Chapter Script

### Act I: Grounding the Asteroid (Pages 1–10)

**Page 1**

- **Panel 1 (Wide Establishing):** A dark, cratered CI carbonaceous asteroid drifts through near-Earth space—the kind sampled by **Hayabusa2** (Ryugu) and **OSIRIS-REx** (Bennu).

- **Panel 2 (Inset):** Inside a laboratory jar, fine charcoal-gray powder gleams under bench lights: **CI asteroid regolith simulant** from UCF's **CLASS Exolith Lab**, engineered to match the **Orgueil** meteorite.

- **Caption:** *Resupplying prepackaged food from Earth won't scale to Mars or the asteroid belt. If crews want fresh crops off-world, they have to test the dirt already floating out there.*

**Page 2**

- **Panel 1:** University of North Dakota Biology greenhouse and `Percival AR-66L` growth chamber. Three seed packets lie side by side: **Outredgeous** romaine lettuce, **Pink Celebration** radish, and **Chimayo** pepper—representing leaf, taproot, and fruit.

- **Panel 2:** A soil-testing report from **Kansas State University** (`Project No. 31005`) spells out the simulant's paradox: `P2O5` (`0.4 wt%`) and `K2O` (`0.4 wt%`) sit in the bulk chemistry, yet plant-available nitrogen (`NO3 1.6`, `NH4 2.5 mg/kg`) and `P` (`2.8 mg/kg`) are starved—well below the `~40 mg/kg` agricultural baseline.

**Page 3**

- **Panel 1:** **Experiment A** layout: `18` `4-inch` pots per randomized complete block (`3` species × `6` substrates). Peat moss (`SunShine` sphagnum) and CI simulant step across five ratios: `100:0`, `75:25`, `50:50`, `25:75`, and `0:100` (`100%` simulant), alongside a commercial topsoil + `9 wt%` vermiculite control.

- **Panel 2:** **Experiment B** adds aeration tests: `50%` simulant / `50%` perlite, `100%` perlite, and topsoil with `25%` vermiculite / `25%` perlite.

**Page 4**

- **Panel 1:** **Experiment C**: Two pots of `5` radish seeds each in `50%` simulant / `50%` perlite mount onto twin **2-D clinostats**—one upright (`1` rotation/day control), one tilted `90°` parallel to the floor (`1 rotation/24 h` simulated microgravity).

- **Panel 2:** Five seeds drop into every pot at `~2.54 cm` (`1 inch`) depth, watered every other day to field capacity with distilled water.

**Page 5**

- **Panel 1:** Inside the `Percival AR-66L`, `32 W` and `14 W` cool white fluorescent tubes plus `25 W` incandescent bulbs ramp smoothly through sunrise, midday (`275 µmol/m²/s`), and sunset across a `16 h / 8 h` cycle (`19.8°C–23.4°C`, `50–60% RH`).

- **Panel 2:** Every other day, distilled water hits the `0:100` pure simulant pots. Instead of draining cleanly like loam, the `68%` silt particles settle and pack tight.

**Page 6**

- **Panel 1 (Close-Up):** As the surface dries between waterings, `Mg-serpentine` clay (`48.0 wt%`) filters upward, hardening into a pale **white crust** across the `0:100` and `50:50` simulant/perlite pots.

- **Caption:** *Day 10. Day 20. In `100:0` and `75:25`, green cotyledons breach the surface. In `0:100`, `50:50` simulant/perlite, and both clinostats—nothing breaks through.*

**Pages 7–8 (Double-Page Spread Anchor): *The Silt Trap***

- **Visual Layout:** A split-soil cutaway at `55 DAP`. On the left (`100:0` & `75:25` peat:simulant), loose, dark organic fibers (`pH 4.7–5.4`, `CEC 35.29–43.98 meq/100 g`) hold air pockets and moisture around vigorous radish taproots, red-tinted `Outredgeous` rosettes, and opposite-leaved `Chimayo` peppers. On the right (`0:100` pure CI simulant), the `68%` silt / `26%` sand / `6%` clay matrix collapses under water into an alkaline (`pH 8.1`), low-`CEC` (`8.15 meq/100 g`), `Mg`-heavy (`6,927 mg/kg`) brick capped by a white serpentine crust. Buried `2.54 cm` deep, ungerminated seeds suffocate from drought stress and lack of oxygen.

- **Anchor Caption:** *Zero germination in `100%` CI simulant, `50:50` simulant/perlite, and the clinostats. Even when watered every other day, compacted silt locks water and air away from the seed.*

**Page 9**

- **Panel 1:** Harvest at **`55 DAP`**. Researchers wash remaining substrate from every surviving root and shoot, lay dried leaves (`60°C` for `64 h`) onto centimeter graph paper, and snap `28 mm` `iPhone SE` photos for `ImageJ` red-threshold leaf-area tracing.

- **Panel 2:** `SAS PROC GLIMMIX` plots the germination curve: across all three species combined, germination drops linearly from `80%` (`100:0` / `75:25`) down to `33%` (`25:75`, `p = 0.0003`).

**Page 10**

- **Panel 1:** Species germination averages: **Radish** leads at `78%`, followed by **Pepper** (`55%`) and **Lettuce** (`53%`).

- **Dialogue (Agronomist):** "Germination tells only half the story. Watch what happens *after* they sprout."

### Act II: Three Crops, Three Fates (Pages 11–22)

**Page 11**

- **Panel 1:** **Radish** (`Raphanus sativus` `"Pink Celebration"`): bright green basal rosettes shrink dramatically as simulant climbs.

- **Panel 2:** Height drops linearly from `4.1 cm` (`100:0`) to `0.65 cm` (`25:75`, `F(1,8) = 32.32`, `p = 0.0005`). Total dry biomass plunges from `95.0 mg` down to `15.4 mg` (`p = 0.0005`).

**Page 12**

- **Panel 1:** Why did radish suffer most? A cross-section of a swelling taproot pushing against dense, low-organic-matter (`14.6%` OM at `25:75` vs. `87.6%` at `100:0`) silt.

- **Caption:** *Taproots demand aeration and phosphorus (`<3 mg/kg` in pure simulant). Compacted serpentine silt starves both.*

**Page 13**

- **Panel 1:** **Romaine Lettuce** (`Lactuca sativa` `"Outredgeous"`): overall height (`1.7 cm` mean) and biomass (`11.8 mg` mean) show no significant treatment shift—until `ImageJ` measures the leaves.

- **Panel 2:** Leaf area shrinks linearly from `2.2 cm²` (`100:0`) to `0.35 cm²` (`50:50`, `p = 0.0191`), and in Block 1 the `50:50` lettuce plants die before harvest.

**Page 14**

- **Panel 1:** **Chimayo Pepper** (`Capsicum annuum`): opposite bright-green leaves stand tall (`3.8 cm` mean height, `0.80 cm²` leaf area, `16.4 mg` biomass), with `50:50` pots actually outperforming lettuce and holding steady across surviving mixes.

- **Dialogue (Researcher):** "Even the `topsoil` control behaved like `≥50%` simulant—similar bulk density, plus `2.54 cm` was simply too deep for tiny lettuce seeds."

**Page 15**

- **Panel 1:** A chemical gradient bar sweeps from `100:0` peat moss to `0:100` simulant:

  - `pH`: `4.7` → `5.4` → `6.2` → `7.0` → `8.1` (alkaline lockup of micronutrients).

  - `CEC`: `43.98` → `35.29` → `33.86` → `23.38` → `8.15 meq/100 g` (leaching risk).

  - `Organic Matter`: `87.6%` → `60.8%` → `35.3%` → `14.6%` → `2.5%`.

**Page 16**

- **Panel 1:** Serpentine soil toxicity check: **Magnesium** climbs from `1,814 mg/kg` (`100:0`) to `6,927 mg/kg` (`0:100`), while **Calcium** drops from `6,028` to `1,780 mg/kg`—inverting the `Mg:Ca` ratio just like terrestrial serpentine barrens.

**Page 17**

- **Panel 1:** Side-by-side comparison table (`Table 5` of the study) pits `CI Simulant` (`P 2.8`, `K 102`, `NO3 1.6`, `pH 8.1`, `CEC 8.15`) against real carbonaceous meteorites **Murchison** (`CM2`, `P 6.0`, `K 650`, `CEC 5.8`) and **Allende** (`CV3`, `P 1,600`, `K 30`, `CEC 0.4`), plus **JSC-1A Lunar** (`P 0.2`, `K 27`, `CEC 9.6`) and **Mars-1A** (`P 0`, `K 138`, `CEC 7.3`).

**Page 18**

- **Panel 1:** Why did plants germinate in `100%` `Mars-1A` and `Mojave Mars` in earlier studies, yet fail completely in `100%` `CI` and `MGS-1`?

- **Panel 2:** Single-source volcanic-ash simulants (`Mars-1A`) vs. multi-mineral synthetic simulants (`MGS-1`, `CI`): high mineral fidelity strips out terrestrial soil microbes and decomposing organic matter, leaving a sterile, crust-prone powder.

**Page 19**

- **Panel 1:** `Experiment B` (`50%` simulant / `50%` perlite) proves perlite alone cannot rescue the crust: white serpentine clay still migrates to the top after watering, sealing the pot shut.

**Page 20**

- **Panel 1:** `Experiment C` (`2D` clinostat) closes with an honest methodological note: because both `1g` upright and `90°` microgravity clinostat pots used `50:50` simulant/perlite and suffered zero germination, `OSD-670` isolates *substrate physics* rather than microgravity effects.

**Page 21**

- **Panel 1:** Looking forward—a blueprint of UND's **NASA-funded Inflatable Lunar/Mars Habitat Plant Module**.

- **Dialogue (Researcher):** "If pure CI regolith is sterile and crusts over, we don't haul chemical fertilizers from Earth. We build a *living* soil—three sisters style."

**Page 22**

- **Panel 1:** Three green-manure cover crops sketch across the blueprint: **Beans** (to fix atmospheric nitrogen into `NO3`/`NH4`), **Squash** (broad leaves to shade the silt and stop surface crusting/drought), and **Corn** (stubble tilled back in to rebuild organic carbon).

### Act III: Returning to Orbit's First Signal (Pages 23–30)

**Page 23**

- **Panel 1:** High-resolution `iPhone SE` morphometric images (`3024 × 4032 px`) and `s_OSD-670` soil tables archive into `LSDS-73` / `OSD-670` (`34.12 MB`), giving future ISRU teams exact `Melich III` and `hydrometer` baselines.

**Page 24**

- **Panel 1:** A dual-horizon closing panel: on one side, asteroid regolith waiting for nitrogen-fixing microbes; on the other, the ISS **Veggie** and **ABRS** racks where *Arabidopsis* first taught us how orbital light and gravity rewrite plant genomes.

**Page 25**

- **Panel 1:** Transition to the orbital archive: *Before crops can thrive in asteroid dirt under artificial LEDs, we still have to settle a foundational question from Low Earth Orbit.*

**Page 26**

- **Panel 1:** Telemetry rewind to **Expedition 39** on the International Space Station. Flight Engineer **Steve Swanson** tends the **CARA** (*Characterizing Arabidopsis Root Attractions*) plates—one set bathed in light, the other sealed in darkness.

**Page 27**

- **Panel 1:** Inside a cold dissection station (`4°C`), `RNAlater`-preserved seedlings thaw. Under a dissecting microscope, fine forceps separate **leaf tissues** from the hypocotyl.

**Page 28**

- **Panel 1:** Two genotypes face off: wild-type **`Col-0`** and the **`phyD`** phytochrome mutant. Earlier **CARA** work (`OSD-120`) showed `phyD` slashed differentially expressed genes in *root tips*—but what happens in the *leaves*?

**Pages 29–30 (Cliffhanger Spread): *Ten Times in the Light***

- **Page 29 (Panel 1):** Side-by-side transcriptome bars erupt from `Col-0` leaves: **light-grown** spaceflight leaves trigger **ten times** as many differentially expressed genes (`DEGs`) as **dark-grown** leaves!

- **Page 30 (Full-Bleed Splash):** Even more startling: key light-signaling, defense, and oxidative-stress pathways flip into *opposite* regulatory directions between light and darkness—proving that light isn't just energy in orbit, it is the principal conductor of the spaceflight transcriptome.

- **Cliffhanger Tag:** *Next in **OSD-678**: Light vs. Dark—and `Col-0` vs. `phyD`—in the orbital leaf transcriptome.*
