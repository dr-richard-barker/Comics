## 1. Volume Metadata Header

- **Series:** *Roots in Orbit: The NASA OSDR Plant Chronicles*
- **Volume:** 73 (Special Chronicles: Gravitropism & Morphogenesis)
- **Chapter Title:** *The Gravity Threshold: Plant Gravity Perception Pilot*
- **OSDR Accession:** `OSD-411` (Plant Gravity Perception: Pilot / PGP Pilot)
- **Primary Publication:** Kiss, J. Z., Medina, F. J., et al. (2019). *Threshold acceleration for plant gravity perception: The European Modular Cultivation System (EMCS) PGP Pilot on the International Space Station*. *New Phytologist*, `224(3):1266-1279` (DOI: `10.1111/nph.16045`).
- **Setting & Hardware:** International Space Station (Columbus Module) — **European Modular Cultivation System (EMCS)**. Variable-speed centrifuge rotors calibrated to generate fractional gravity levels: 0g (microgravity hub), `0.001g`, `0.01g`, `0.05g`, `0.1g` (Lunar threshold), and `1.0g` (terrestrial control). Sealed Experiment Containers with automated video imaging and LED photostimulation.
- **Organism & Assay Scope:** *Arabidopsis thaliana* wild-type Col-0 vs. starch-deficient phosphoglucomutase mutant (*pgm1-1*). Whole-seedling transcriptomics, root curvature angles, statolith displacement kinematics, and cell cycle mitotic index in columella and meristematic zones.

## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **Squad Col-0 (Dense Amyloplast Statoliths):** Wild-type seedlings packed with heavy, starch-filled amyloplasts that tumble against the endoplasmic reticulum even at minuscule acceleration levels.
- **Squad PGM1 (The Starchless Floaters):** *pgm1-1* mutants with empty, low-density plastids. They require nearly ten times higher g-forces to trigger the mechanosensitive threshold.
- **The Perception Sentinels (`PIN3`, `PIN7`):** Auxin efflux carriers positioned at the plasma membrane of columella cells, ready to re-localize when amyloplasts contact the ER meshwork.
- **The Centrifuge Architects (Dr. John Z. Kiss & Dr. F. Javier Medina):** International research leads directing the EMCS variable rotors from ground control centers in Columbus and ESTEC.
- **The Gravitropic Relay (`LAZY1`, `LZY2`, `LZY3`):** Key signaling transducers passing the physical contact signal to the lateral root flanks.

## 3. 32-Page Chapter Script

### Act I: Calibration of the Centrifuge (Pages 1–10)

**Page 1**
- **Panel 1 [Wide Shot / Technical Blueprint]:** The EMCS centrifuge assembly inside Columbus. The dual counter-rotating rotors are spun up to precise angular velocities: `ω = 0.5 rad/s`, dialing in fractional g-levels down to the milligravity regime.
- **Panel 2:** A cutaway of the Experiment Container (EC). Seeds embedded in dry filter paper are hydrated by remote command from ground control.
- **Caption (Narrator):** *How much gravity does a plant need to know which way is down? On Earth, 1g is all they have ever felt. In the EMCS centrifuge, we dissect the perception threshold milligravity by milligravity.*
- **Dialogue (Flight Controller):** "Hydration valves opened on Rotor A. Temperature nominal at 22.0°C. Centrifuge speed locked to 0.05g."

**Page 2**
- **Panel 1:** Columella cell interior of wild-type Col-0. High-density starch grains (amyloplasts) feel the tiny pull of `0.05g`.
- **Panel 2:** The amyloplasts sink slowly through the actin filament meshwork, coming into contact with the lower peripheral endoplasmic reticulum.
- **SFX:** *TING-SHHH (Microscopic sedimentation)*
- **Dialogue (Dr. Medina):** "At 0.05g, the amyloplasts are settling! Look at the actin tension vectors—the mechanosensitive ion channels are opening!"

**Page 3**
- **Panel 1:** Split screen comparing Col-0 with *pgm1-1* under `0.05g`. In Col-0, the heavy starch grains sediment against the wall; in *pgm1*, the light amyloplasts remain suspended in the cytosol like dust motes in space.
- **Panel 2:** The Col-0 root initiates a downward curvature angle of 14 degrees. The *pgm1* root continues growing straight forward, blind to the `0.05g` acceleration vector.
- **Caption:** *Without starch, the weight of the plastid drops below the thermal noise threshold.*

**Page 4–10**
- Testing the critical threshold ladder: `0.001g` (no response) → `0.01g` (weak transient response) → `0.05g` (definitive biological perception in Col-0).
- Lunar gravity simulation (`0.16g`): Col-0 displays full, robust gravitropic curvature identical to terrestrial 1g.
- Martian gravity simulation (`0.38g`): Both Col-0 and *pgm1* display complete downward reorientation, proving that Mars provides more than sufficient gravity for normal plant rooting architecture!

### Act II: The Molecular Gravity Switch (Pages 11–20)

**Page 11–15**
- Transcriptomic analysis of seedlings exposed to the threshold curve.
- Immediate-early gene activation within 15 minutes of threshold perception: *TCH2*, *TCH4* (touch/mechanosensitive genes) spike rapidly.
- *PIN3* and *PIN7* auxin transporters polarize to the lower lateral membranes of the columella, generating the classic Cholodny-Went auxin gradient along the lower root flank.

**Page 16–20**
- In starchless *pgm1*, the activation threshold is shifted by a factor of 10: it requires `0.3g` before plastid mass overcomes cytosolic resistance.
- Cell cycle regulation in the root apical meristem: *CYCB1;1* and *CDKB1;1* maintain active division even at `0.01g`, proving that cell division is uncoupled from gravity perception.

### Act III: The Threshold Ledger (Pages 21–32)

**Page 21–25**
- Synthesis of video tracking data: root tip curvature rate (degrees per hour) plotted against applied g-force.
- Discovery: The minimum threshold for plant gravity perception is between **`0.01g` and `0.03g`**.
- Lunar gravity (`0.16g`) and Martian gravity (`0.38g`) are well above the threshold of perception, confirming that lunar and Martian agricultural colonies will not suffer from microgravity root wandering!

**Page 26–30**
- The engineering takeaway: future space greenhouse centrifuges do not need to generate full 1.0g terrestrial gravity to maintain healthy plant morphology. A gentle `0.2g` rotation is sufficient to establish 100% directional root growth, drastically reducing centrifugal mechanical mass and power consumption.

- Infographic spread: The Gravitational Threshold Curve and Columella Sedimentation Dynamics.
