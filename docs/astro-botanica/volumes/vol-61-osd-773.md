## 1. Volume Metadata Header

- **Series:** *Roots in Orbit: The NASA OSDR Plant Chronicles*

- **Volume:** 61

- **Chapter Title:** *Roots in Thin Air*

- **OSDR Accession:** `OSD-773` (`GeneLab ID: GLDS-676`, Version 2 | `DOI: 10.26030/y0ng-8r17` | License: `CC0-1.0` | Size: `4.69 GB` | Submitted: `13-Sep-2024` | Initial Release: `11-May-2026` | Updated: `10-Jul-2026`)

- **Primary Publication:** Khodadad, C. L. M., Spern, C. J., Hummerick, M. E., Gooden, J. L., Morales, C. J., Wheeler, R. M., Melendez, O., Morrow, R., Wetzel, J., & Zhang, Y. (2026). *Microbial community characterization of multi-crop growouts in the XROOTS aeroponic–hydroponic system on the International Space Station*. *Frontiers in Microbiomes*, `5:1779816` (`15-Jun-2026`, `DOI: 10.3389/frmbi.2026.1779816`) — Frontiers | PubMed: 42376193

- **Skipped Accessions:** None (`OSD-773` immediately follows `OSD-772`)

- **Hardware & Environment:** International Space Station (`ISS-Columbus`) **eXposed Root On-Orbit Test System (`XROOTS`)** integrated beneath **Veggie** (baseplate and bellows removed; launched `NG-17` `19-Feb-2022`, returned `SpaceX-26` & `SpaceX-27`); aeroponic misting + ebb-and-flow hydroponic nutrient recovery (`2` root module configurations, `3` root/shoot interface designs, watering/recovery every `3` days; `26°C`, `16:8` light:dark RGB LEDs).

- **Organism & Assay Scope:** Multi-crop grow-outs `2–4` (June–October 2022): radish (*Raphanus sativus* cv. `'Cherry Belle'`), lettuce (*Lactuca sativa* cv. `'Little Gem'`), mizuna mustard (*Brassica rapa*), dwarf wheat (*Triticum aestivum*), and transplanted dwarf pea + tomato (`84` DAI fruiting); `n = 156` samples (`MELFI` `-80°C`); `TSA`/`IMA` plate counts & pathogen screening, plus **`16S`** (`V2 500-cycle`) and **`ITS`** (`Zymo Quick-ITS Plus`, `V3 500-cycle` `250/251 bp`) `Illumina MiSeq` amplicon sequencing.

## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **Dr. Ye Zhang (NASA KSC Conceptualization Lead):** Principal investigator guiding `XROOTS` biological validation—bridging microgravity fluid physics with edible multi-crop food safety.

- **Dr. Christina L. M. Khodadad (Noetic Strategies Microbiologist):** Lead author of the *Frontiers in Microbiomes* analysis; compares `XROOTS`'s un-sanitized, substrate-free multi-crop microbiome against historical Veggie pillow baselines.

- **Cory Spern, Mary Hummerick, Jennifer Gooden, Cristiana Morales, Orlando Melendez & Raymond Wheeler:** KSC science and microbiology team running `70 µm` filtration, `OMNI` bead-ruptor extractions, `TSA`/`IMA` plating, and `16S`/`ITS` `MiSeq` libraries.

- **Robert Morrow & John Wetzel (Sierra Space `XROOTS` Hardware Leads):** Engineers behind the capillary root modules, mist nozzles, and ebb-and-flow recovery loops—supported by Dan Barta, Marlin Cox, Douglas White, Guillermo Tellez, and Daniel Wyman.

- **The Exposed-Root Canopy (`6` Crops Across `4` Grow-Outs):** `'Cherry Belle'` radish, `'Little Gem'` lettuce, mizuna mustard, dwarf wheat, and `84`-day fruiting dwarf pea and tomato—their bare roots suspended in `0 g` air and mist.

- **The Un-Sanitized Multi-Crop Microbiome (`Pseudomonas`, `Trichoderma`, *Fusarium*, *Blumeria graminis*):** High-diversity communities seeded without surface sterilization (`65` unique leaf and `87` unique root bacterial genera vs. earlier Veggie tests), where `Pseudomonas` and `Trichoderma` keep opportunistic fungi in check.

## 3. 32-Page Chapter Script

### Act I: Unbolting the Baseplate (Pages 1–10)

**Page 1**

- **Panel 1 (Full-width establishing shot):** `NG-17` Cygnus approaches the International Space Station on February 19, 2022. Inside `Columbus`, astronauts unbolt the standard Veggie baseplate and bellows.

- **Panel 2 (Hardware integration):** Beneath the hanging Veggie RGB LED array, crew members mount the transparent **`XROOTS`** root-module chassis—developed by Sierra Space under NASA's NextSTEP program.

- **Caption (Narrator):** *For eight years, NASA grew space crops inside clay pillows. `XROOTS` rips the pillows away—leaving roots naked in microgravity.*

- **Panel 3 (Two-shot, KSC & Madison Payload Ops):** Ye Zhang and Robert Morrow watch live engineering cameras lock onto the clear root modules.

- **Ye Zhang:** "Two root-module configurations, three root-shoot interfaces, aerosol misting, and ebb-and-flow fluid recovery. Let's see how roots drink without gravity or clay."

**Page 2**

- **Panel 1 (Close-up, seed cartridge):** Unlike Veggie and `PH-04` (`OSD-772`), the `XROOTS` seed cartridges are loaded with **un-sanitized** seeds—a deliberate operational choice to see what a multi-crop, soil-free system carries on its own.

- **Panel 2 (Crew operation):** Every three days, an astronaut cycles the nutrient delivery and ebb-and-flow water recovery valves. Mist plumes swirl briefly inside the chamber before capillary channels draw excess droplets back into the reservoir.

**Page 3**

- **Panel 1 (Grow-out 2, `40` DAP):** Inside the `26°C`, `16:8` RGB LED glow, three crops share the `XROOTS` modules: **`'Cherry Belle'` radish**, **`'Little Gem'` lettuce**, and **mizuna mustard** (*Brassica rapa*).

- **Panel 2 (Root close-up):** White taproots and fine lateral root hairs hang suspended in misted air—no `Turface` arcillite, no `Grodan` rockwool—while radish bulbs swell cleanly at the root-shoot collar.

**Page 4**

- **Panel 1 (Grow-out 3 & Transplant into Grow-out 4):** Next come **dwarf wheat** (*Triticum aestivum*) and lettuce—and beside them, **dwarf pea** and **dwarf tomato** seedlings that are carefully transplanted into a fresh root module for Grow-out `4`, stretching all the way to **`84` days after initiation (`DAI`)**.

- **Panel 2 (Crew pollination):** Remembering `PH-04`'s lessons, ISS crew members flick and brush the yellow tomato and pea blossoms by hand— coaxing pods and red cherry tomatoes to set fruit in bare-root hydroponics.

**Page 5**

- **Panel 1 (Harvest & `MELFI` packing):** Leaves, roots, radish bulbs, pea pods, tomato fruits, wicks, nutrient solution syringes, and `ProSan`-wipe root-module swabs (taken before and after sanitization) are wrapped in sanitized foil and frozen at **`-80°C`** in `MELFI`.

- **Panel 2 (Return vehicles):** `SpaceX-26` and `SpaceX-27` carry the frozen `XROOTS` archive (`n = 156` samples) back to Kennedy Space Center.

**Page 6**

- **Panel 1 (KSC thawing & dissection):** Christina Khodadad, Cory Spern, and Mary Hummerick dissect the `XROOTS` harvests: distal upper leaves separated from proximal lower leaves, wicks, and bare roots.

- **Panel 2 (Detail, folded wheat leaf — *Frontiers* Fig. 2):** A tall dwarf wheat leaf had folded back inside its foil packet during return, touching its own lower blade—a reminder to document every physical contact point when interpreting microbial counts.

**Pages 7–8 (Double-Page Spread Anchor: *Inside the Substrate-Free Loop*)**

- **Left Page (Page 7) — *Six Crops, Zero Clay (`OSD-773` / `GLDS-676`)*:** A cutaway of the `XROOTS` dual-chamber system: mist nozzles and ebb-and-flow channels feeding bare roots of `'Little Gem'` lettuce, mizuna, `'Cherry Belle'` radish, dwarf wheat, pea, and tomato (`84` DAI). Callouts mark the exact microbial checkpoints: `nutrient solution (65–3,800 CFU/mL bacteria; 25–660 CFU/mL fungi)`, `RM1–RM2 surface swabs`, `proximal vs. distal leaves`, `wicks`, and `edible fruits/pods`.

- **Right Page (Page 8) — *Dual-Amplicon Extraction (`16S` + `ITS`)*:** Tissue homogenates (`OMNI Bead Ruptor`, `4 mm` glass beads, `5 m/s`) pass through **`70 µm` cell strainers**, spin at **`13,000 × g`** for `3 min`, and enter `Qiagen DNeasy Microbial` DNA isolation—splitting into `16S` (`V2 500-cycle` `MiSeq`) and `Zymo Quick-ITS Plus` (`V3 500-cycle` `MiSeq`, `250/251 bp`).

- **Spread Caption:** *No soil to hide in. Every microbe in `XROOTS` rides the mist, clings to the bare root, or swims through the ebb-and-flow loop.*

**Page 9**

- **Panel 1 (Culture plate readout):** On `TSA` and `IMA`, bacterial and fungal counts in the nutrient solution range from **`65` to `3,800 CFU/mL`** (bacteria) and **`25` to `660 CFU/mL`** (fungi)—peaking in Harvest `3` (`RM1`).

- **Panel 2 (Tissue gradient):** Across every crop, **distal (upper) leaves** carry lower bacterial counts than proximal leaves, wicks, and roots—and **tomato fruits and pea pods** record the lowest microbial loads of all.

**Page 10**

- **Panel 1 (Food-safety clearance):** Every screen for *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, and *Staphylococcus aureus* comes back **negative**; edible leaf and fruit counts sit squarely within Earth-grown produce ranges and prior Veggie leafy-green datasets.

- **Christina Khodadad:** "Even without solid clay or seed sanitization, `XROOTS` crops pass food-safety muster."

### Act II: Un-Sanitized Diversity & The Hidden Mold (Pages 11–22)

**Page 11**

- **Panel 1 (`16S` Diversity Comparison):** Jennifer Gooden and Cristiana Morales line up the `XROOTS` `16S` profiles against historical `2017–2018` Veggie grow-outs (`VEG-03D/E/F`).

- **Panel 2 (Contrast numbers):** Alpha diversity is still lower in leaves than in roots—but `XROOTS` carries far more unique bacterial genera: **`65` unique genera in leaf tissue** and **`87` unique genera in root tissue**, sharing only `5` leaf and `26` root genera with earlier Veggie missions!

- **Ye Zhang:** "Un-sanitized seeds plus a shared multi-crop water loop opened the door to a much richer microbiome."

**Page 12**

- **Panel 1 (Stacked bar plots):** Across the majority of `156` samples, **`Pseudomonas`** claims the highest relative abundance.

- **Panel 2 (Four-crop leaf Venn diagram — *Frontiers* Fig. 7A):** Comparing leaves of leafy greens (lettuce + mizuna), tomato, radish, and pea, **only `6` bacterial genera** are shared across all four crop types: `Pseudomonas`, `Achromobacter`, `Stenotrophomonas`, `Sphingobium`, `Rhizobium`, and `Rhodococcus`.

- **Panel 3 (Leafy-green uniqueness):** Meanwhile, lettuce and mizuna alone harbor **`48` of `70`** total leaf genera not seen in tomato, radish, or pea—including `Burkholderia`, `Mesorhizobium`, and `Sphingomonas`.

**Page 13**

- **Panel 1 (`ITS` Fungal Sequencing alert):** Orlando Melendez and Cory Spern open the `V3 500-cycle` `ITS` fungal tables.

- **Panel 2 (Two flagged names on screen):** Opportunistic *Fusarium* appears—and alongside it, **`Blumeria graminis`** (cereal powdery mildew), detected for the **first time ever on the ISS** on co-grown wheat, tomato, and pea in Grow-out `3/4`!

- **Cory Spern:** "Wait—*Blumeria graminis* powdery mildew on orbit? Did the plants crash?"

**Page 14**

- **Panel 1 (Photo check, `XROOTS` canopy):** High-resolution ISS stills show crisp green wheat, healthy pea pods, and smooth red tomatoes—**zero visible plant disease**.

- **Panel 2 (Microbial balance inset):** Co-occurring **`Pseudomonas`** and **`Trichoderma`** species—known natural antagonists of *Fusarium* and fungal pathogens—saturate the rhizosphere and phyllosphere.

- **Raymond Wheeler:** "In a living, un-sanitized community, beneficial competitors can hold latent pathogens in check. Still—now we know *Blumeria* can ride up on un-sanitized seeds."

**Page 15**

- **Panel 1 (Hardware hygiene note):** Swabs from the top of the Veggie bellows show higher bacterial and fungal counts than the bottom of the bellows—highlighting where condensation and airborne spores settle in `0 g`.

- **Panel 2 (Two-shot):** John Wetzel and Robert Morrow log the engineering lessons: `XROOTS` proved bare-root aeroponics/hydroponics works in microgravity from seed to fruiting pea and tomato (`84` DAI) while cutting launch mass.

**Page 16**

- **Panel 1 (Publication milestone):** `OSD-773` (`GLDS-676`, `DOI: 10.26030/y0ng-8r17`, `4.69 GB`) and the June 15, 2026 *Frontiers in Microbiomes* paper (`PMID: 42376193`) cement `XROOTS` as the orbital baseline for substrate-free multi-crop farming.

**Page 17**

- **Panel 1 (Reflective beat, KSC):** Raymond Wheeler looks back at the Veggie rack in the SSPF high bay.

- **Raymond Wheeler:** "In `XROOTS`, we tested `6` crops under one light recipe. But before `XROOTS` ever flew—back in `2019`—we ran a very different test on **light itself**."

**Page 18**

- **Panel 1 (Color spectrum shift):** The white-pink `XROOTS` glow splits into two distinct spectral recipes: **red-heavy** vs. **blue-enriched** Veggie light arrays.

- **Caption (Narrator):** *Next in the OSDR archive, we rewind to `2019`—when `VEG-04A` and `VEG-04B` asked whether tuning red, blue, and green LEDs changes not just how mizuna mustard grows, but how it **tastes** and what microbes ride its leaves.*

### Act III: The Spectrum of Flavor (`OSD-780`) (Pages 19–30)

**Page 19**

- **Panel 1 (Establishing shot):** Two ISS **Veggie** units (`VEG-04A` and `VEG-04B`, `2019`) glow side by side with contrasting red-to-blue-to-green LED formulations, growing **mizuna mustard** (*Brassica rapa* var. *japonica*).

- **Panel 2 (Astronaut evaluation):** Crew members harvest, weigh, and complete **organoleptic taste evaluations** in orbit—rating bitterness, pungency, texture, and freshness—while parallel ground controls run at KSC.

**Page 20**

- **Panel 1 (Contrast preview):** Flight vs. ground mizuna samples (`n = 207` total samples in `OSD-780`) show differences in yield, chemistry, and light-treatment response—and bacterial and fungal plate counts run **lower on ground** than in spaceflight!

**Page 21**

- **Panel 1 (KSC archive):** `OSD-773` (`XROOTS`, `156` samples) closes cleanly in OSDR (`10-Jul-2026`), linking aeroponic fluid loops to the Veggie hardware heritage.

**Page 22**

- **Panel 1 (Detail):** A bare `'Cherry Belle'` radish taproot from `XROOTS` Grow-out `2` sits beside a `0.5 ng/µL` `16S` PCR tube—proof that `0 g` aeroponics can feed both `16S` and `ITS` surveillance cleanly.

**Page 23**

- **Panel 1 (Visual bridge):** Droplets of `XROOTS` nutrient mist dissolve into individual photons—`455 nm` blue and `630 nm` red—striking serrated mizuna mustard leaves.

**Page 24**

- **Panel 1 (Hardware continuity):** Veggie (`2014`) → `VEG-04A/B` (`2019` light quality) → `APH PH-04` (`2021` chile peppers) → `XROOTS` (`2022` bare-root multi-crop).

**Page 25**

- **Panel 1 (Microbiology link):** On `XROOTS` (`OSD-773`), mizuna carried `48` unique leaf bacterial genera alongside lettuce. In `VEG-04A/B` (`OSD-780`), mizuna takes center stage alone under two competing light recipes.

**Page 26**

- **Panel 1 (Organoleptic scorecards):** Clipboards in `Columbus` record crew flavor scores alongside harvest mass—connecting photon ratios directly to astronaut appetite.

**Page 27**

- **Panel 1 (Plate count bench):** Plant leaves, roots, and Veggie hardware surface swabs line up for `TSA` and `IMA` plate-count assays (`n = 207` samples) to map how light formulation and microgravity shape microbial load.

**Page 28**

- **Panel 1 (Data bridge):** `OSD-773` (`XROOTS`) → `OSD-780` (`VEG-04A`/`VEG-04B` mizuna mustard): from bare roots in mist to the physics of flavor and light.

**Pages 29–30 (Cliffhanger Double-Page Spread: *Next Station — `OSD-780`*)**

- **Right Page (Page 30):** Analytical preview for **`OSD-780`**: `VEG-04A` & `VEG-04B` (`2019`), red-to-blue-to-green light formulations, astronaut organoleptic taste tests, nutritional chemistry, and `n = 207` plant/hardware surface microbial plate counts.
