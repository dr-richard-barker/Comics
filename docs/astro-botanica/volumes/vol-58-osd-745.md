## 2. Dramatis Personae (Grounded in Study Data)

- **Dr. Gioia Massa (KSC Veggie Project Lead):** Conceptualization lead for Veggie hardware validation (`VEG-01A`, `VEG-01B`, `VEG-03A`); orchestrates the transition from Earth-bound plant-pillow engineering to the first astronaut-consumed salad in orbit.

- **Dr. Christina L. M. Khodadad (KSC / LASSO Microbiologist):** First author of the *Frontiers in Plant Science* synthesis (`PMC7067979`); leads the microbiological food-safety clearance and nutritional/antioxidant profiling across all three Veggie harvests.

- **Mary Hummerick, LaShelle Spencer & Anirudha Dixit (KSC Crop & Bioinformatics Team):** Co-authors driving heterotrophic plating, `Illumina MiSeq` `16S`/`ITS` community sequencing, `iCAP 7000` ICP-OES elemental digestion, and `ASE 350` phytochemical extractions alongside **Jeffrey Richards**, **Matthew Romeyn**, **Trent Smith**, and **Raymond Wheeler**.

- **The Orbital Harvesters (Steve Swanson, Scott Kelly, Kjell Lindgren, Shane Kimbrough):** ISS crew members who tend the `6` rooting pillows, thin seedlings at Day `7`, execute sterile scissor harvests, consume half of `VEG-01B` and `VEG-03A`'s sequential cuts, and pack the foil-wrapped return leaves at `-80°C`.

- **The `'Outredgeous'` Red Romaine Canopy:** *Lactuca sativa* cv. `'Outredgeous'`—carrying anthocyanins, phenolics, and mineral nutrients across `VEG-01A`, `VEG-01B`, and the multi-week "cut-and-come-again" ordeal of `VEG-03A`.

- **The Phyllosphere & Rhizosphere Consortia:** Non-pathogenic leaf-surface bacteria (`2.14–4.86 log₁₀ CFU/g`, zero *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, or *S. aureus*) and root-pillow Proteobacteria (`Burkholderia`, `Ralstonia`, `Janthinobacterium`) and Ascomycota (`Microidium`, `Alternaria`, `Phaeoacremonium`).

## 3. 32-Page Chapter Script

### Act I: Before the First Bite (Pages 1–10)

**Page 1**

- **Panel 1 (Full-width orbital shot):** Sunlight rakes across the International Space Station's `Columbus` module. Inside, a soft magenta glow leaks through the bellows of the **Vegetable Production System (Veggie)**.

- **Panel 2 (Close-up):** Six heat-sealed, gas-impermeable `Tedlar` bags open inside the crew work area, revealing six flat rooting pillows packed with porous `Turface Proleague` arcillite and `Nutricote 18-6-8` controlled-release fertilizer.

- **Caption (Narrator):** *May 8, 2014. `SpaceX-3` has docked. Before an astronaut can legally swallow a single leaf grown in microgravity, NASA has to prove the salad won't make them sick.*

- **Panel 3 (Detail):** Two surface-sanitized `'Outredgeous'` seeds sit glued with guar gum between paired germination wicks inside each pillow. A syringe injects `25 mL` of water into the wick.

**Page 2**

- **Panel 1 (Two-shot, KSC SSPF):** Gioia Massa and Christina Khodadad watch the telemetry link from `ISS-Columbus` inside the Kennedy Space Center control room.

- **Gioia Massa:** "`VEG-01A` is live on orbit. `30.5` to `31.75` centimeters below the LEDs—`200 µmol/m²/s` PAR, `12` red, `3` blue, `1` green, `16` hours on, `8` off."

- **Panel 2 (Control chamber inset):** Twenty-four hours behind orbit, a parallel ground-control Veggie unit hums inside a KSC environmental chamber, replaying the ISS temperature (`~23.25°C`) and humidity curve.

- **Christina Khodadad:** "Twenty-four-hour lag for `VEG-01A` and `01B`. Seventy-two hours for `VEG-03A`. Every leaf they freeze upstairs gets a twin on Earth."

**Page 3**

- **Panel 1 (Day 7 montage):** In microgravity, Steve Swanson uses fine forceps to thin each pillow to a single `'Outredgeous'` seedling.

- **Panel 2 (Day 33, `VEG-01A`):** June 10, 2014. Sanitized scissors snip the mature `33`-day leaves. Every leaf is wrapped in aluminum foil and sealed inside the `-80°C` orbital freezer—none eaten yet.

- **Panel 3 (Wide KSC bench):** July 8 – August 10, 2015: **`VEG-01B`** blooms in orbit under Scott Kelly and Kjell Lindgren. Down on Earth, a `NIKON D4S` (`105 mm`, `f/14`) documents the ground-control pillows day by day (`LSDS-109` photography).

**Page 4**

- **Panel 1 (Iconic orbital beat):** August 10, 2015. Scott Kelly and Kjell Lindgren float beside the `VEG-01B` harvest, wiping `'Outredgeous'` leaves with food-safe sanitizing wipes before tasting the first NASA-grown space crop—while the remaining leaves head to `-80°C` for Khodadad's lab.

- **Panel 2 (Timeline jump):** October 25 – December 28, 2016 (`SpaceX-8` era): **`VEG-03A`**. Shane Kimbrough tends the pillows not for a single `33`-day clear-cut, but for **cut-and-come-again** sequential harvesting.

- **Shane Kimbrough:** "First cut at Day `33`, then three more weekly harvests. We eat the first, third, and half the fourth—and send the second harvest plus the rest of the fourth home frozen."

**Page 5**

- **Panel 1 (Wide KSC lab):** Freezer boxes from `VEG-01A`, `VEG-01B`, and `VEG-03A` (`n = 60` leaf samples in `OSD-745`, plus root pillows) arrive at KSC. Christina Khodadad and Mary Hummerick pull on nitrile gloves.

- **Panel 2 (Close-up, agar plates):** Leaf and root homogenates hit heterotrophic plate count media for bacteria, yeast, and mold—alongside selective pathogen screens for *Escherichia coli*, *Salmonella* sp., and *Staphylococcus aureus*.

- **Mary Hummerick:** "First gate is food safety. If *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, or *S. aureus* shows up on those leaves, the whole program pauses."

**Page 6**

- **Panel 1 (Plate counts readout):** Colony counters click across `VEG-01A`, `VEG-01B`, and `VEG-03A` harvests: leaf bacterial counts average **`3.53`**, **`4.86`**, **`2.89`**, and **`2.14 log₁₀ CFU/g`**; fungal counts sit between **`2.3`** and **`4.3 log₁₀ CFU/g`**.

- **Panel 2 (Negative screens):** Every selective pathogen plate—*E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *S. aureus*—comes back **completely negative**, corroborated by `Illumina MiSeq` `16S`/`ITS` amplicon sequencing.

- **Christina Khodadad:** "Lower bacterial loads than typical farm or market produce—and zero target human pathogens."

**Pages 7–8 (Double-Page Spread Anchor: *The Dual Ledger — Orbit vs. Earth*)**

- **Left Page (Page 7) — *The Living Canopy (`16S` & `ITS` Microbiome)*:** A split botanical silhouette of `'Outredgeous'`—roots anchored in porous arcillite below, ruffled burgundy-green leaves unfurling above. `80–96%` Proteobacteria (`Burkholderia`, `Ralstonia`, `Janthinobacterium`), `Chitinophaga`, and `Thermogemmatispora` populate the root zone (`10⁶–10⁸ CFU/g`) with far higher Shannon diversity than the leaves, while `Ascomycota` (`92–99%`: `Microidium`, `Alternaria`, `Phaeoacremonium`) dominate the fungi. Only `VEG-03A` shows a significant flight-vs-ground shift in leaf bacterial genera.

- **Right Page (Page 8) — *The Nutritional Assay (`OSD-745`)*:** Freeze-dried `'Outredgeous'` leaf powder feeds four parallel analytical lines: the `Dionex ASE 350` (`1500 psi`, `80–100°C`), the `Beckman DU 700` spectrophotometer (`530`/`650 nm`), the `BioTek Synergy Hybrid` microplate reader (`Folin-Ciocalteu` & `ORAC-FL`), and the `Mod Block` + `iCAP 7000` ICP-OES plasma torch.

- **Spread Caption:** *Safe to swallow is only half the bar. If microgravity strips the minerals or antioxidants out of the leaf, a fresh salad is just crunchy water.*

**Page 9**

- **Panel 1 (Detail, `ASE 350` cell):** LaShelle Spencer packs `100 mg` of dry leaf powder into an `ASE 350` extraction cell for anthocyanins (`MWA` `85:14.5:0.5`, `100°C`, `1500 psi`, `5 min` static), while `25 mg` aliquots enter parallel cells for phenolics and `ORAC` (`AWA` `70:29.5:0.5`, `80°C`, `1500 psi`).

- **Panel 2 (Spectrophotometer readout):** `Beckman DU 700` measures absorbance at `530` and `650 nm` against a **Cyanidin-3-Glucoside** standard curve.

- **Panel 3 (Result):** Across `VEG-01A`, `VEG-01B`, and `VEG-03A`, flight and ground anthocyanin levels show **no significant difference**.

**Page 10**

- **Panel 1 (Microplate setup):** On the `BioTek Synergy Hybrid` reader (`37°C`, `2 h` decay), `20 µL` of `1/50`-diluted `AWA` extract meets `200 µL` fluorescein and `20 µL` `AAPH` peroxyl radical generator in a forward-then-reverse `96`-well layout (edge wells skipped to block plate effects), calibrated to **Trolox** equivalents (`µmol TE/g` DW).

- **Panel 2 (Curve overlay):** Decay curves for flight and ground leaves overlap tightly.

- **LaShelle Spencer:** "ORAC antioxidant capacity didn't budge either. Spaceflight didn't dull the radical-scavenging power."

### Act II: The Phenolic Dip & The Elemental Ledger (Pages 11–22)

**Page 11**

- **Panel 1 (Colorimetric tubes):** `0.2 mL` aliquots react with **Folin-Ciocalteu** phenol reagent against a **gallic acid (GA)** calibration curve (`BioTek Synergy Hybrid`).

- **Panel 2 (Bar chart):** `VEG-01A` and `VEG-01B` hold high total phenolics—but **`VEG-03A`** drops significantly (`p ≤ 0.001`), averaging **`15.0 mg/g` DW (ground)** and **`19.6 mg/g` DW (flight)**.

- **Christina Khodadad:** "Repeated weekly clipping in `VEG-03A`—or its longer `56`-day run—changed the phenolic baseline across the crop."

**Page 12**

- **Panel 1 (Acid digestion hood):** `0.2–0.5 g` of dried, ground leaf tissue sits in `5 mL` of `70%` trace-metal-grade `HNO₃` on a `95°C` graphite `Mod Block` (EPA Method 3050B mod.), boiling clear for `2 h` before `2.5 mL` `30% H₂O₂` foams and clears, then filtering through `0.45 µm` into `50 mL` ultra-pure DI water.

- **Panel 2 (`iCAP 7000` ICP-OES):** Sample mist hits the argon plasma.

- **Panel 3 (Two-shot):** Jeffrey Richards and Matthew Romeyn scan the elemental table.

- **Jeffrey Richards:** "Within any single planting, flight and ground match. No microgravity mineral penalty."

**Page 13**

- **Panel 1 (Cross-experiment comparison chart):** Comparing *across* the three plantings, the `iCAP 7000` reveals sharp inter-mission shifts: **`Fe`** (`p ≤ 0.001`) and **`K`** (`p ≤ 0.0001`) are significantly lower in `VEG-03A` than in `VEG-01A` or `VEG-01B`.

- **Panel 2 (Second chart):** Meanwhile, **`VEG-01B`** spikes higher in **`Na`** (`p ≤ 0.0001`), **`P`** (`p ≤ 0.05`), **`S`** (`p ≤ 0.05`), and **`Zn`** (`p ≤ 0.0001`), while **`Ca`**, **`Mg`**, and **`Mn`** stay flat across all three tests.

- **Raymond Wheeler:** "Hardware watering, pillow blend (`100%` `600 µm–1 mm` vs. `1:1` `1–2 mm`), and harvest schedule drive more mineral variance than microgravity itself."

**Page 14**

- **Panel 1 (Microbial detective work):** Anirudha Dixit pulls up the `Illumina MiSeq` (`V2 500-cycle`) taxonomic bars (`PMC7067979` Figure 4).

- **Panel 2 (Comparison):** In `VEG-01A` and `VEG-01B`, `80–96%` of bacterial reads are Proteobacteria (`Burkholderia`, `Ralstonia`, `Janthinobacterium`), with `Chitinophaga` higher in flight roots (except `VEG-01B` ground).

- **Panel 3 (Highlight on `VEG-03A`):** In `VEG-03A`, nitrogen-fixing `Azospirillum` and `Herbaspirillum` rise, `Thermogemmatispora` drops, and—for the first time—**flight vs. ground leaf bacterial genera diverge significantly**.

**Page 15**

- **Panel 1 (Fungal bar chart):** `Ascomycota` accounts for `92–99%` of fungal reads; `Basidiomycota` hits `7%` in `VEG-01A` flight roots and `6%` in `VEG-01B` flight roots (`≤2%` elsewhere).

- **Panel 2 (Microscopic silhouettes):** Obligate biotroph `Microidium` dominates leaves alongside `Alternaria`, `Malassezia`, and `Fusarium` (`VEG-01B` only), while roots harbor `Alternaria`, `Phaeoacremonium`, and `Rhodosporidiobolus`, and culturable `Rhodotorula` appears in almost every sample except `VEG-03A` leaves.

- **Mary Hummerick:** "Root diversity is always richer than leaf diversity—and every culturable isolate showed up in the sequencing too."

**Page 16**

- **Panel 1 (Wide KSC conference room):** The full Veggie team—Massa, Khodadad, Hummerick, Spencer, Dixit, Richards, Romeyn, Smith, Wheeler—reviews the integrated `VEG-01A`/`01B`/`03A` verdict.

- **Panel 2 (Checklist):**

  - Human pathogens: **0**

  - Leaf CFU counts: **Low (`2.14–4.86 log₁₀ CFU/g`)**

  - Anthocyanins & ORAC: **Maintained in space**

  - Flight vs. Ground minerals: **Equivalent within each mission**

- **Gioia Massa:** "Veggie works. Astronauts can grow and eat `'Outredgeous'` on the ISS safely, repeatedly, and with full antioxidant value."

**Page 17**

- **Panel 1 (Data curation terminal):** Years after the 2020 paper, the `OSD-745` (`LSDS-109`) dataset is standardized by the **ALSDA Analysis Working Group** into machine-readable row/column templates—linking `60` leaf samples, `NIKON D4S` photography, `Beckman DU 700` anthocyanins, `BioTek` phenolics & `ORAC`, and `iCAP 7000` ICP-OES elements under `DOI: 10.26030/4tsr-7518`.

- **Panel 2 (Close-up):** `OSD-745` goes live on NASA OSDR (`22-Dec-2025`), completing the analytical loop with `OSD-267`, `OSD-268`, and `OSD-269`.

**Page 18**

- **Panel 1 (Reflective beat):** Christina Khodadad looks at a printed photo of Scott Kelly holding a red romaine leaf in `Columbus`.

- **Christina Khodadad:** "Salads are morale and micronutrients. They're not calories."

- **Panel 2 (Wide shot):** Beyond the SSPF windows, the Space Coast sky fades to indigo. On a nearby whiteboard, Artemis lunar-surface mission timelines replace ISS orbital tracks.

- **Raymond Wheeler:** "On the Moon, nobody's shipping `25`-milliliter arcillite pillows for every meal. You need starch. You need tubers. You need **local dirt**."

### Act III: From Orbital Salad to Lunar Dust (Pages 19–30)

**Page 19**

- **Caption (Narrator):** *Lettuce proved that spaceflight crops can be safe and nutritious. Now the frontier shifts from microgravity greenhouses to planetary surfaces—and to the staple crop built for high harvest index.*

**Page 20**

- **Panel 2 (Labels on the jars):** Two lunar **mare** simulants (including `LMS-1E`) and three lunar **highland** simulants (including `LHS-1E`, `NUW-LHT-5M`, `OPRH4W30`)—plus `JSC-1A` and **vermicompost** (worm castings) at `0%`, `15%`, and `30%`.

**Page 21**

**Page 22**
